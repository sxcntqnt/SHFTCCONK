Most Rongai commuters set their alarm for 5am not because they want to, but because they are afraid. Afraid of traffic. Afraid of being late. Afraid of the uncertainty that comes with not knowing what the road will do today.

That fear costs you an hour of sleep every morning — and over a working year, that is roughly **250 hours** lost to anxiety, not commuting.

## The actual time windows that matter

The Rongai–CBD route has three distinct morning windows:

**Window 1: 5:15am – 6:00am**
Road is clear. Journey takes 35–45 minutes. You arrive in CBD before most offices open. You sit in a coffee shop or your car. You wait.

**Window 2: 6:00am – 7:15am**
This is the sweet spot. Vehicles are full but moving. Journey is 50–65 minutes. You arrive between 7:10 and 8:20am — right on time for a normal start.

**Window 3: 7:15am – 8:30am**
Everything changes. The Ongata Rongai–Bomas stretch backs up. Magadi Road near Karen slows. Journey stretches to 90 minutes or more. This is where "I'll just leave a bit later" becomes a 9:30am arrival.

## The time management technique that changes this

The **time management matrix** — popularised by Stephen Covey — separates tasks into urgent vs important. Your commute timing falls into the same trap most people fall into with their work: you react to urgency instead of planning for importance.

Planning your departure window the night before is an important, non-urgent task. Most people skip it. Then they wake up reactive and spend the morning in survival mode.

**What to do instead:**

1. Check the community board the evening before for any reported roadworks or diversions on Magadi Road
2. Decide your departure time the night before — not when your alarm goes off
3. Set a hard "latest leave" time of 6:45am and protect it the way you would protect a meeting

## The goal

Stop leaving at 5am out of fear. Start leaving at 6:20am with intention.

That extra 80 minutes in bed, or at home with a proper breakfast, is not laziness. It is the difference between arriving at work ready and arriving already depleted.
