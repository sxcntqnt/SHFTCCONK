Thika Road has a reputation. Commuters who live along it — Kasarani, Githurai, Roysambu, Garden City — treat it like a weather system: unpredictable, powerful, to be feared.

But Thika Road is actually one of the more readable routes in Nairobi if you understand its patterns.

## What Thika Road actually does in the morning

The road moves in phases, and most commuters only ever experience one of them because they leave at the same time every day out of habit.

**Before 6:30am:** Road is fluid. Vehicles from Githurai 45 and Kasarani stage move fast. CBD in under 40 minutes.

**6:30am – 7:30am:** Volume builds at Allsops and the Pangani roundabout. Journey expands to 60–75 minutes. Still manageable.

**7:30am – 9:00am:** The Garden City, Muthaiga, and UN roundabout sequence becomes the chokepoint. Journey is now 90–120 minutes. This is where most Kasarani commuters get caught.

## The smarter approach

Board at **Kasarani stage or Hunters**, not mid-route at Roasters or Garden City. Boarding at the origin gives you a seat, a calmer start, and predictable journey time.

If your job starts at 8am, your target departure is **6:45am at the latest**. If your job starts at 9am, **7:15am is your window** — not 7:45am when panic kicks in.

## Connect this to your broader time management strategy

One of the most practical time management techniques for Nairobi professionals is **reverse scheduling your morning from your first meeting**, not forward from your alarm.

If your first meeting is 9am:
- You need to be at your desk by 8:40am
- You need to arrive CBD by 8:20am
- You need to board by 7:00am
- You need to leave home by 6:45am
- You need to wake up by 6:00am

Work backwards. The alarm time is the last decision you make, not the first.
