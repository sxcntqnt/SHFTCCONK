Here is a perspective that changed how I think about my commute.

Nairobi traffic is not going to be fixed this year. Probably not in five years. The BRT project will help eventually. New roads will be built eventually. But right now, in 2026, the road infrastructure is what it is.

Which means the only variable you actually control is **when you leave and how prepared you are when you do**.

## Time management and commuting are the same problem

The importance of goal setting in professional life is that it forces you to work backwards from outcomes, not forwards from effort.

Most Nairobi commuters plan their commute forward:
> "I will wake up at 6am and see how the road feels."

High-performing professionals plan it backward:
> "My first meeting is at 9am. I need to be at my desk at 8:40am. That means CBD arrival by 8:15am. That means I board by 6:50am. That means I leave home at 6:30am. That means my alarm is 5:55am."

The difference is not discipline. It is **a system**.

## What the time management matrix tells us about commuting

The Eisenhower matrix — urgent vs important — is one of the most well-known time management techniques. It tells you to focus on important, non-urgent work, not just what feels urgent.

Applied to commuting:

- **Urgent and unimportant:** Scrambling to find a matatu after you have already missed your window
- **Urgent and important:** Leaving now because you are already late
- **Not urgent, not important:** Watching news on your phone while standing at a crowded stage
- **Important, not urgent:** Checking community reports the evening before and deciding your departure window

Most commuters live in the first two quadrants. The professionals who consistently arrive calm and on time live in the fourth.

## The commute is 2–3 hours of your day

For a five-day-a-week commuter from Rongai, Kasarani, or Thika, the commute is roughly 2–2.5 hours daily. That is 500–625 hours per year. More than three full working months.

What you do with that time — mentally, emotionally, practically — determines whether commuting is a tax on your life or a resource.

Use Matatu Pulse to make those hours intentional. Read the community before you leave. Know what you are walking into. Arrive as a professional who planned their morning, not as someone who survived it.
