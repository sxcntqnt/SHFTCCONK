I have been commuting from Syokimau to the CBD for eight months now. I leave at 6am, I am usually at my desk by 7:45am, and by 10am I am already running low.

I am not sleeping badly. I am eating breakfast. But something about the commute itself is draining me before I even open my laptop.

Is this just the cost of living far from the office, or is there something I am doing wrong?

---

**Top community response — Grace A. (Verified Rider):**

What you are describing is not a transport problem. It is an energy management problem that happens to occur during transport.

The commute exhausts people not because of the distance, but because of the **mental load** it carries. When you board a matatu uncertain about whether you will arrive on time, your brain runs a low-grade stress response for the entire journey. Cortisol stays elevated. You arrive having already spent mental energy managing anxiety rather than resting.

**Three things that made the biggest difference for me:**

**1. Decide everything the night before.**
Know your departure time. Know which stage you are boarding from. Know what you will do if traffic is heavy. When you wake up with a plan, the morning brain does not need to problem-solve. Problem-solving is expensive energy.

**2. Protect the commute itself.**
Use the journey as transition time, not work time and not doom-scrolling. Audiobooks, a podcast you actually enjoy, or silence with earphones in. The commute is 45–90 minutes of time you cannot use for work — so stop trying to, and stop resenting it. Use it to arrive mentally prepared.

**3. Read ground reports before you leave, not after you are already stuck.**
Checking Matatu Pulse community reports at 5:45am — before you commit to leaving — means you board already knowing what the road looks like. That certainty is calming. Boarding uninformed is stressful.

The mindfulness meaning here is not sitting cross-legged. It is arriving at each moment of your morning with intention rather than reaction.

**Response — David K.:**

Also worth checking: are you eating or drinking anything on the matatu? Blood sugar drops during a long commute make everything feel worse. A banana and water before you board changes the physiology of the journey more than most people expect.
